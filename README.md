## Overview

This project analyzes the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset to understand factors associated with employee attrition and to develop machine learning models for predicting whether an employee is likely to leave an organization. The project covers end-to-end data analytics: data understanding, cleaning, exploratory data analysis (EDA), feature engineering, model training, evaluation, and interpretation.

---

## Problem Statement

Employee attrition imposes significant financial and operational costs on organizations. Identifying employees at risk of leaving — before they do — allows HR teams to take proactive measures. This project investigates which workplace and demographic factors are associated with attrition and builds a binary classification model to predict the attrition outcome.

---

## Objectives

1. Perform thorough data understanding and quality assessment of the IBM HR dataset.
2. Clean and preprocess the dataset for analysis and machine learning.
3. Conduct exploratory data analysis to identify patterns and associations with attrition.
4. Develop and evaluate machine learning models (Logistic Regression and Random Forest).

## Methodology

### Data Cleaning
- Removed 3 constant columns (`EmployeeCount`, `Over18`, `StandardHours`) and the identifier column (`EmployeeNumber`).
- No missing values or duplicate rows were found.
- Numerical outliers were reviewed; no erroneous values requiring removal were identified.

### Exploratory Data Analysis
- Analyzed attrition distribution, demographics, employment characteristics, compensation, satisfaction ratings, and work conditions.
- Visualizations include attrition distribution, attrition by department, job role, overtime, marital status, business travel, and correlation heatmaps.

### Feature Engineering
- One-hot encoding applied to multi-category categorical features.
- Binary encoding applied to `OverTime` and `Attrition`.
- Stratified 80/20 train/test split (random_state=42).
- StandardScaler applied for Logistic Regression (fit on training set only).
- Class imbalance addressed using `class_weight='balanced'` in both models.

## Evaluation Metrics

Models are evaluated using:
- **Accuracy** — overall correct predictions
- **Precision** — of employees flagged as leaving, how many actually leave
- **Recall** — of employees who actually leave, how many are correctly identified
- **F1-Score** — harmonic mean of precision and recall
- **ROC-AUC** — overall discriminative ability across all thresholds

> **Note:** In attrition prediction, Recall and ROC-AUC are prioritized over Accuracy alone due to class imbalance.

## Key Findings

1. **Attrition rate:** 16.12% of employees (237 out of 1,470) left the organization.
2. **Overtime** is the most prominent factor associated with attrition in both EDA and model feature importance.
3. **Entry-level (Job Level 1) employees** show the highest attrition rate among all job levels.
4. **Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians** have the highest attrition rates by job role.
5. **Single employees** show higher attrition rates than married or divorced employees.
6. **Younger employees and those with fewer years at the company** are disproportionately represented among those who leave.
7. **Lower monthly income** is associated with higher attrition.
8. **Frequent business travelers** exhibit higher attrition than non-travelers.
9. Both models' full metric results (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, ROC-AUC) are computed and displayed when the notebook is executed. See Section 14 of the notebook for the model comparison table.
10. Top predictive features (from model outputs): `OverTime_Binary`, `MonthlyIncome`, `Age`, `TotalWorkingYears`, `MaritalStatus_Single`, and job role indicators.

## Limitations

- The dataset is synthetically generated and may not reflect real organizational dynamics.
- Findings are associative — causation cannot be established from this observational data.
- The dataset represents a single organizational snapshot; results may not generalize.
- Class imbalance (16% attrition) requires careful interpretation of metrics beyond accuracy.
